# anghel4d.com

Personal site for Matei Anghel, served at anghel4d.com. One static `index.html`, no build step, no package manager.

## Layout of index.html

1. `<head>`: the link-preview tags (between `meta:begin` and `meta:end`, written by `tools/meta/build.js`; see Link previews), an inline SVG favicon (the lion) and the fonts, all our own (no third-party stylesheet). The first view's two faces are inside the page as base64 WOFF2 between the `fonts:begin` and `fonts:end` markers: "Newsreader Statement" (Newsreader italic fixed at weight 400 and optical size 40, the statement's only face) and "JetBrains Mono" (weight 400, without code ligatures). The menu's and panels' faces are files, `assets/fonts/newsreader-italic.woff2` (weights 300 to 400, every optical size) and `newsreader-roman.woff2`, fetched 1.5 s after the first view is up. All four hold Basic Latin and French only; `tools/fonts/build.py` rebuilds them from Google Fonts and rewrites the inline block. Licences: `licenses/newsreader-OFL.txt`, `licenses/jetbrains-mono-OFL.txt`. three.js r147 is not in the head: the boot script fetches it from jsdelivr only for the three.js renderer (the fallback), so it never blocks the page. Post-processing is our own; no `examples/js` helpers are loaded.
2. `<style>`: colour tokens on `:root`, light and dark sets (dark via `prefers-color-scheme` and `[data-theme="dark"]`). `--scene` is the gradient shown before the first frame and whenever neither renderer can draw; it mirrors the rendered scene in each theme.
3. Markup: corner controls (lion mark top left, menu top right, en/fr bottom left, day/night bottom right), the statement, four panels (`#work`, `#about`, `#blog`, `#contact`) opened over a blurred veil, and the poise gauge (`#poise`, below). The blog has a tab row under its heading, in the controls' mono: Technical and Culture greyed out (`aria-disabled`), Singularity Koans the one open tab; the tabs are copy (`T.en.tabs`, `T.fr.tabs`). The posts are Matei's own words in Markdown, one file each in `blog/posts/`: a single element whose name is its tab (`<koan date="2026-10-07">`; a tab's `id` is that name) holding the Markdown as written. English only, and shown exactly as written in both languages; only the date line follows the page's language. `blog/blog.js`, the master handler, reads them and renders the Markdown: a `# ` title, `##` and `###` headings, paragraphs, `*italic*`, `__underline__`, `~~struck through~~` (Matei's marks) and `[links](https://…)`; anything else stays as written. The page loads it and the posts 1.5 s after the first view is up, or as soon as the blog opens, from the list in the address map; a file that is gone is skipped. Each post is a row (a + or − mark, the title, the date) that unfolds like an addendum (`<details>`, its body growing into place, folding back the same way); unfolding one shows its address, `#blog/` plus the title as a slug, and an address naming a post unfolds it. `date` sets a post's date and its place (newest first). Adding, changing or removing a post means rerunning `node tools/meta/build.js` (the deploy does it). An open tab with no posts shows its `…Empty` line.
4. UI script: `T.en` / `T.fr` copy, addresses (on anghel4d.com real paths, `/work/`, `/blog/<post>/`, which opens the blog at that post; elsewhere, a local preview or a copy of the page, the part after `#`: `#work`, `#blog/<post>`; links carry the address, and the page keeps its title and preview tags in step with it, `setMeta()`, from the address map at the end of the page), the theme (day or night: the system's light or dark setting, or the host page's `data-theme`, picks the first once as the page loads; the button then switches between the two, and nothing is stored), and `window.A4D_STATE`, which the scene reads (`night`, `targetNight`, `pointer`, `open`). It brings the first view up (`html.ready`: the statement fades in over 0.6 s) as soon as the statement's face is decoded, 300 ms at most, and resolves `window.A4D_SHOWN`, which everything else waits for. Poise: the ocean (the first view, where the statement leans instead of a panel), the menu, Work, About, Blog and Contact stand in a column (`ORDER`), so scrolling down from the first view leads into the menu and scrolling up from the menu back to the ocean. At the bottom of one (the ocean and the menu fit on the screen, so they are at both ends), scrolling on (wheel, a drag up, Page Down, Space, the down arrow) fills a hairline gauge under "next · about" while the panel leans up a little; at the top, scrolling up (a drag down, Page Up, the up arrow) fills one at the top edge, "previous · work", while the panel leans down. It drains when the scrolling stops; full, the panel moves on (in 0.17 s) and its neighbour comes in from the other side, at its end when going back. About 460 px of wheel, a 135 px drag or two key presses fill it. It arms only on a fresh gesture made at the edge, so the momentum of the scroll that reached the edge cannot fill it. Nothing comes before the ocean or after Contact. Keyboard: `nav.js` (loaded once the first view is up) turns vim's hjkl and the arrow keys into one `a4d:nav` event, `{ dir, edge }`, so both sets of keys do the same thing. j and k (down, up) scroll the layer a little and, at its edge, lean on the gauge (two presses go through); with Shift they are edge steps, to the top or bottom and from there straight to the neighbour (landing at its top going on, its end going back). h and l (left, right) step the focus through the layer's links, tabs and posts. Page Up and Down, Home, End and Space keep the browser's scrolling and lean at the edge.
5. Lagoon core (`lagoon()`): what every renderer shares, as plain data: `LOOK`, the sea's constants, both spectra and their slope statistics (`SLOPE`, `SLOPE2`), the noise table, the caustic and environment sizes, GT7's constants and the framing (`HORIZON`, `PHONE_F`, `GLINT_EL`). A renderer destructures what it uses. It is computed on the first call, when a renderer starts (about 0.1 s of spectra on a desktop), not while the page is parsed.
6. The three.js renderer (WebGL2), `startGL()`: the fallback, run by the boot script where WebGPU is missing or fails, or with `?three` in the address. It is the reference the WebGPU renderer matches, and where the scene is described below. The water follows [CAUSTIC//VOLUME](https://github.com/ScottieFox/caustic-volume) (MIT, `licenses/caustic-volume.txt`); the FFT pass, the height-field intersection and the B-spline filter come from [Clearwater](https://github.com/Aureliengmz/clearwater) (MIT, `licenses/clearwater.txt`); the tone mapping is GT7's, ported from Polyphony Digital's sample implementation (MIT, `licenses/gt7-tone-mapping.txt`). three.js is a thin layer here: every pass is a GLSL3 `RawShaderMaterial` drawn into a `WebGLRenderTarget`. A full-screen pass first invalidates its target (`gl.invalidateFramebuffer`), so a tiled GPU (every phone) does not load what the target held: about 35 MB a frame at the phone's size, most of it the FFT's 26 passes. All tunables (depth, water optics, sand, sun, glints, capillary roughness, glitter density, sky brightness, exposure, grade, defocus, haze, speed) live in the `LOOK` block; colours belong there, not scattered through the shaders.
   - **Sea**: two FFT cascades, summed. The chop: a JONSWAP spectrum with a cross swell, 256x256 over a 4.2 m patch (`L`), transformed on the GPU each frame, made choppy by horizontal displacement, then inverted into a periodic texture of height and slope (mipmapped). The swell (`LOOK.swell`), the open sea beyond the calm lagoon: it blends in between 10 and 20 m, so the close-up stays as calm as the chop alone. Seen only that far out, it can be coarse: 32x32 over 61.44 m, so the two never share a period, holding only waves from 8 m up, peaked at 12 m and narrow in direction, rolling in toward the viewer so that far out its crests lie along the horizon. Only its slopes are used, taken exactly in the spectrum, so one FFT gives them; one trilinear read per far pixel adds them to the chop's. Dispersion is quantised so the surface repeats every 2 minutes. Near the camera a rotated copy of the chop at 2.6x scale hides its tiling; from 8 m out (`LOOK.tiling`) the chop's patch is tiled stochastically (Mikkelsen's hex tiling): hexagonal cells one patch wide, each showing the patch at a random offset, turned by up to 20 degrees, blended with weights that keep the slope variance. Fine noise ripples are added close to the camera.
   - **Caustics**: one photon per vertex of a 192x192 grid laid over the patch, refracted to a horizontal plane; intensity is the ratio of the flat-water landing area to the actual one (`dFdx`/`dFdy`), accumulated additively. The grid is a window a little wider than the patch (`CAUS_EDGE`, 24 cells each side), slid per pass by whole cells to where flat water sends the light from, so each photon that lands in the map is drawn once: the same output as a 3x3 tiling for a fifth of the vertices. The bed map (1024x1024) runs one pass per channel (IOR 1.3310 / 1.3340 / 1.3380) for dispersion and is focused at `LOOK.dbed`; deeper bed reads it defocused. Eight more slices from the surface to 3 m (`DVOL`), four to a texture, form the caustic volume.
   - **Water**: one full-screen pass. Rays above the horizon shade the sky and stop. Below it, a fixed-point intersection with the height field, the reflection of the sky (an equirect environment map, refreshed twice a second, with 4x anisotropic filtering) and of the glint sun at every scale (below), refraction into the water, in-scattering along the ray (the caustic slices exaggerated against their own blurred average make the light shafts, `LOOK.god`), and the bed: rippled white sand, lit by the bed caustics, Beer-Lambert attenuated (`LOOK.sigA`, `LOOK.sigS`). The bed shelves away from the viewer (`LOOK.shelf`), so near water is bright turquoise and far water turns deep blue. The far sea goes behind the same haze as the cloud bank's base (`HAZE`, 0.45 at the horizon, built up over `LOOK.fog` metres), so the two meet at the horizon. Noise is read, not hashed: the bed's fixed value noise from a lattice the GPU hashes once at startup with the shaders' own `hash12` (`hashRT`, exact to half-float precision), grain and the ripples' noise from a periodic noise texture whose second channel holds the next time slice. The in-scattering loop multiplies its attenuation step to step instead of calling `exp`.
   - **The sea at every scale**: the filtered slope map loses the slope variance of waves finer than a pixel's footprint, which along the view is stretched by 1 / sin(depression). Past about 5 degrees below the horizon more than half is gone, and shading the flattened normal turns the far sea into a mirror (Fresnel near 1, reflecting the haze band). The shader puts the lost variance back with no extra texture data:
     1. **Lost variance, in closed form.** Along and across the view, from each cascade's spectrum (`SLOPE`, `SLOPE2`: the slope covariance, and the lost share g(w) = x^p / (1 + x^p) with x = w / s50, p fitted to the spectrum: 1.65 for the chop, 2.75 for the narrower swell) and the footprint. The effective filter width (0.48 x footprint, with a 0.8-texel floor) and the share the map keeps at its finest (0.72) were measured against the real pipeline (re-measured with the hex tiling: unchanged within noise). The swell is read at the mip level of the footprint along the view, so its filter is isotropic, about 0.6 x that footprint (mip box plus trilinear), with bilinear's 0.41 texel at the finest; its variance enters in proportion to its blend, squared. Capillaries finer than the FFT (`LOOK.capillary`) join in the same proportion.
     2. **Smith masking.** For Gaussian slopes, the exact mean and spread of the visible facets' tilt, carried by two facets, give the expected Fresnel and the sky those facets reflect. The sky is sampled with an anisotropic blur: along the view, twice the tilt spread; across it, that times cos(i), so far reflections streak vertically.
     3. **Glints.** The resolved facet's disc hands over, by the share still resolved, to the glitter of unresolved facets. That glitter is the microfacet mean (Gaussian slopes, Smith masking), drawn as a Poisson count of sparkles from `LOOK.facets` per square metre, re-drawn about 12 times a second. By day, where many facets share a pixel (toward the horizon, below the sun), the glitter merges into a bright sheen, kept as is. By moonlight only the resolved facets glint (the unresolved glitter is scaled by 1 - night): the moon path is the near sparkles and ends in the dark water. Kept at night, that sheen made a pool of light at the horizon; pressed down, a grey band; drawn as sparse sparkles, a strip pasted on the horizon. All three were rejected.

     Near the camera nothing changes. If the spectrum, the slope pass or the texture filtering changes, re-measure 0.48, 0.8 and 0.72. Render the filtered slopes along and across the view into a float target, then fit their per-row variance against distance.
   - **Sky**: a vivid cyan-blue gradient that pales toward the horizon, a haze band, a cumulus bank on the horizon (towers by azimuth, billowed edges, sunlit tops, blue-grey bases) behind the shared haze (`HAZE`), sparse high clouds and wisps, the glint sun's glow, and at night the moon and stars. The moon is a photograph of the full moon (`assets/moon.webp`, 256 x 256, cropped to its disc, the image Matei supplied), fetched when the scene starts (flat grey until then) and mipmapped; `moonFace()` lays it on a sharp-edged disc of radius `LOOK.moon.size` (0.028 rad, about six times the real moon's, so its face reads), its highlands near paper white at night's exposure (`LOOK.moon.bright`) so the seas and rays show, with `LOOK.moon.sat` of its cool colour kept. At night the eye looks at the moon: the final pass drops the defocus within two moon radii of it (full again from four), so the moon is sharp and has no colour rim. Its brightness (`LOOK.sky`) is set so the sky reads as the light the water is lit by.
   - **Post**: HDR all the way to the tone mapper, SDR out. Bloom pyramid at a low weight; then, in the final pass:
     1. **Haze and defocus as one blur.** With the camera fixed above a flat sea, both depend only on the pixel's elevation, computed from the camera ray (nothing stored per pixel).
        - **Defocus** (`LOOK.myopia`): a slightly short-sighted eye, sharp to its far point, then a blur disc that grows with 1/far − 1/distance to 0.3 degrees across at the horizon (in green), 40% more at night (the dilated pupil). The eye's own chromatic error is longitudinal, so it lives here, not as colour pushed toward the frame's corners: each channel has its own far point (red 3.25 m, green 3 m, blue 2.6 m; blue's dioptric offset twice red's, as in the eye), so far off blue's disc is a little wider than red's, as in the duochrome test. Near the camera every channel is sharp.
        - **Haze** (`LOOK.haze`, fake but shaped like the real thing): a hazy layer's optical depth at elevation e goes as 1/(sin e + s₀), capped at the horizon. With s₀ setting the half-height (3 degrees above the horizon, 1 below), the scattered share is f = 1 − exp(−3 s₀ / (|sin e| + s₀)). It becomes an airlight veil toward the horizon colour, a light softening, and extra bloom as forward-scatter glow; all stronger by day.
        - **One lookup.** The two Gaussian blurs add in quadrature, and a single read serves both: the HDR frame's mip pyramid (built by the GPU) through a cubic B-spline in four bilinear taps per level. Each channel sits at its own level and blends its two nearest, so the three channels share two levels, or three where they straddle one (at most 12 taps). From 0.15 to 0.6 px of blur a channel blends from the frame itself to level 0; below 0.15 px the pixel is one tap. Each level is graded before the blend (the eye comes after the scene's colours): graded afterwards, the night grade turned the blue rims of bright glints magenta.
     2. **Exposure** (`LOOK.exposure`: scene to frame-buffer units, 1.0 = 100 nits) and vignette.
     3. **Grade** (`LOOK.grade`): one matrix on linear RGB that scales chroma (1.35 by day, 2.4 at night) and turns it 15 degrees at night, so the blues go royal while neutrals and the moon stay white. Calibrated so GT7 keeps the old look (median ΔE 4.3 by day, 3.2 at night). Applied inside the blur (above), to the bloom, and to the airlight on the CPU; with one blur for all channels this is the same as grading after.
     4. **GT7 tone mapping**, SDR mode: Rec.2020, the GT curve per channel (toe, linear mid-tones, shoulder converging on a 250-nit paper white), its ICtCp intensity kept with the input's chroma (fading near the peak), blended 40/60.
     5. Back to Rec.709, the sRGB curve, and grain. No temporal accumulation, no lateral colour fringes (a lens artifact, not the eye's) and no lens-diffraction glare.
   - **Runtime** (both renderers): the water's clock is real elapsed time times `LOOK.speed` (1, true speed for these waves), the same on every device and at any frame rate. Frames are capped at 30 a second, paced against a running target so any refresh rate averages 30. Under a panel the scene holds its last frame once the veil is up (0.7 s) and any change of theme has settled (`sceneHeld`), so the page's motion has the GPU to itself; a resize or a return to the tab draws again for a moment (`sceneWake`). Performance target: the GPU finishes a frame in under about 55% of the 33 ms budget. Resolution: 1.2 render pixels per CSS pixel on every screen (`PX_PER_CSS`, the phone's density), times an adaptive `quality` between 0.5 and 1. The WebGPU renderer knows each frame's GPU time (`onSubmittedWorkDone`, counted from the submission or from the end of the frame before, whichever is later). After three draws in a row with the median of the last five over 80% of the budget, or with the last frame still running, it sets `quality` straight to what a cost model says fits half the budget: a fixed part (the sea, the caustics: `FIXED_PX`, about 130,000 full-scale pixels' worth by the shader-cost estimates) plus the pixels, as `quality` squared. A slow device settles within a few frames, and the GPU is never more than two frames behind. The three.js renderer's fence resolves only to a display refresh, so three late frames step `quality` down by a fifth. Both creep back up by 8% after three seconds of comfortable frames (WebGPU: only if the model says the larger frame fits). Rendering pauses when the tab is hidden. Without WebGL2, renderable float targets, three.js, or a shader that compiles, the page keeps the `--scene` gradient (`html.no-gl`). The canvas fades in (`html.gl`) only after the first good frame.

7. The WebGPU renderer, `startGPU(canvas)`: the default. The same picture as the three.js renderer, its shaders the WGSL twins of the GLSL ones, drawn with compute where it pays and with less work where the picture cannot show it. Whole frames agree with the three.js renderer within 4/255 on the water, and within 6/255 in the sky (fine high-cloud detail near the horizon, under 1% of sky pixels off by more than 2). Each frame: one compute pass for the sea (each FFT line in workgroup memory, the spectrum evolved as it loads; the choppy surface inverted and its slopes taken in one tiled kernel; the swell; the mip chains), a quarter of the sky map when one is due, the visible sky at half resolution, the caustics, one compute pass for the maps' mips and the volume texture, the water, one compute pass for the HDR frame's mips and the bloom, and the final pass to the canvas.
   - **Sky at half resolution** (`skyWGSL`): the final pass blurs the sky by at least 1.7 pixels (defocus and haze) and reads it from the HDR frame's mip levels 1 and 2, so full-resolution sky shading is lost. One texel per 2x2 block of screen pixels, shaded for the ray through the block's centre; the water pass reads it back bilinearly. What is sharp the water pass draws itself at full resolution: the moon's disc, the stars, and the cumulus bank's edge, marked by the bank's mask in the texture's alpha (read back between 0 and 1: about 2% of sky pixels). Sky pixels cost about a tenth of what they did.
   - **Volume texture** (`volWGSL`): each caustic slice beside its mip-3 blur (r and g) in one 256x256x8 3D texture, so a step of the in-scattering loop is one trilinear read; the hardware does the interpolation between slices that the loop did by hand.
   - **Sky map**: refreshed twice a second, a quarter at a time over four frames (scissored bands), so no frame carries the whole of it; in full, every frame, while the theme changes.
   - **Rows**: every offscreen map keeps WebGL's row order (row 0 at the bottom of the view), so the WGSL reads exactly as its GLSL twin: the offscreen full-screen triangle and the caustic vertex shader flip clip y, and only the final pass turns the picture for the canvas.
   - **Caustics**: drawn as three.js draws them, back faces culled (`cullMode: "back"`, `frontFace: "cw"`, since the rows run flipped). The triangles a fold of the light turns over are left out; drawing them adds about 6% to the bed's light.
   - **Uniforms**: one `Frame` struct per frame (offsets in `uniforms()`), one 256-byte slot per caustic draw.
   - **Mip chains** hold only the levels the shaders read: the bed map to level 6, the slices to level 3 (the volume texture's blur), the HDR frame to level 6 (`HDR_LEVELS`; the final pass clamps to the chain). The views each frame draws into are made once.
   - **Timing**: where the browser grants `timestamp-query`, each pass group (sea, sky map, sky, caustics, maps, water, post, final) is timed on the GPU and shown in `#diag`.
   - **Cost**: `tools/shader-cost` compiles the page's WGSL with AMD's shader compiler (no GPU needed) and prints each pass's instructions, registers and estimated cycles; see its README. At the phone's size the frame came to about 49M wave64-cycles on RDNA2 (the water 27M, the final pass 8.9M, the caustics 6.7M, the sea 2.9M, the half-resolution sky 2.1M): under a millisecond on a Radeon 680M, before memory and rasterisation.
   - **Failure**: a validation or out-of-memory error on the first frame, or a lost device later, hands over to the three.js renderer on a fresh canvas (`window.A4D_FALLBACK`).
   - Some WGSL needs care to compile everywhere: the water shader turns off the derivative uniformity check (`diagnostic(off, derivative_uniformity)`), since its quad-uniform branches are uniform in practice but not provably (WebKit's compiler rejects it without); a reversed `smoothstep` is written as `1 - smoothstep` with ordered edges.
   - **Safari**: `tools/safari-wgsl` compiles the page's WGSL with WebKit's own compiler, as Safari does, on Linux (see its README). A module Safari rejects sends iPhones to the three.js renderer without a word. As of October 2026 all 13 pass, with no warnings, on WebKit main and on the Safari 26 and 27 release branches. Safari's other paths checked against WebKit's source: `onSubmittedWorkDone` resolves from Metal's completion handler (the frame timing holds), and `createImageBitmap`'s resize options and `copyExternalImageToTexture` into `rgba8unorm-srgb` both work (the moon). Every limit the renderer needs is a WebGPU default.

8. Boot script: the scene on its own thread. The lagoon core and both renderers are `<script type="text/x-lagoon">`, text the page does not run. Once the statement has faded in (`A4D_SHOWN`, then its `transitionend`, 900 ms at most), the boot runs them in a worker (a Blob of a small stand-in for the page plus their text) that draws on the canvas handed over to it (`transferControlToOffscreen`), so the scene's work never touches the page's thread: scrolling, taps and the panels stay instant whatever the scene costs. The stand-in gives the scripts what they read of the page (`innerWidth`/`innerHeight` and `resize`, `document.hidden` and `visibilitychange`, `A4D_STATE`, the html element's classes, the canvas, `requestAnimationFrame`); messages keep it in step (the page's `open`, `targetNight` and `pointer` go there through setters on `A4D_STATE`; `night`, which the scene eases, and the stats come back twice a second, with the `gl`/`no-gl` classes). WebGPU where the worker has it; otherwise, or on any failure, or with `?three`, the three.js renderer (fetched with `importScripts`) on a fresh canvas the page hands over. The same scripts run on the page instead where a canvas cannot go to a worker, where the worker fails before its first frame, where only the page has WebGPU, and with `?inline` (the shader tools use it, to see the WGSL). Until the first frame the page shows `--scene`.

9. Link previews (`tools/meta/build.js`): what Discord, X, Slack, iMessage and the rest show when a link is shared. Their bots read a page's tags without running it and never see the part after `#`, so every address that can be shared is a real path with a small page of its own: `/menu/`, `/work/`, `/about/`, `/blog/`, `/contact/` and `/blog/<post>/` (`<dir>/index.html`, which any static host serves at `/<dir>/`). Each holds its title, its opening words (up to 200 characters, cut at a word; a post's first paragraph as written) and the picture, then hands over to `/#<address>`, which shows the path again. The picture is `assets/card.jpg`: the scene alone by night, no text, 1201x742 (a golden rectangle) with the moon on its golden section (0.382 across, 0.236 down), cut from a 1600x900 view rendered at twice the render density without grain, clock fixed, the three.js renderer's final frame read back and downsampled in linear light, JPEG at quality 90 without chroma subsampling (99 KB; lossless would be 374 KB as WebP, 471 KB as PNG). Previews show it large (`twitter:card` summary_large_image), with the night colour as the accent (`theme-color` #071824; the page sets it to the theme in use as it loads). Its address carries a hash of the file (`?v=`), so previewers that keep pictures fetch a changed one. The tool writes the head's tags, these pages (removing those of posts that are gone) and the address map (`routes:begin` and `routes:end`, which also lists the posts' files) from the page's own copy and the posts, read through `blog/blog.js`. Both build tools read the site through `tools/site.js` (the page, its copy and views, `blog/blog.js` and the posts), which refuses a post whose element is not a tab, without a date, or with another post's title.

10. Diagnostics script: off unless the address ends in `#diag` (or `window.A4D_DIAG` is set), so visitors pay nothing. It shows the device and viewport, the render settings (`A4D_STATE.stats`, filled by the scene), the frame rate, the water clock against real time, the renderer and adapter, the GPU time of each pass (WebGPU with timestamp queries), the GPU and its float formats, and a float-readback test of the glint maths on that GPU. The copy button works in WebKit: `execCommand("copy")` on a selected read-only textarea inside the tap, then `navigator.clipboard`, then the text shown selected in a box. The readout stops refreshing while text in it is selected. Its controls exist in English and French; the readout is data in one fixed format, so readouts from different devices compare line by line.

11. The raw page (`raw/index.html`, written by `tools/raw/build.js`, which `tools/meta/build.js` runs): the whole site in HTML and CSS alone, no script, no rendering. The same tree as the page (corner controls, statement, veil, the menu and the four panels, drawn by the page's own `VIEWS`, the block between `views:begin` and `views:end`, which the page uses too), its styles copied in, then made minimal (`tools/raw/raw.css`): no scene and no picture, the palette's flat ground (mist by day, `#071824` by night), and nothing moving or blurred (no transitions, animations, filters or glow). CSS does what script does on the page: the address is the router (`:target`; `#work`, `#blog/<post>`; nothing targeted is the ocean); every piece of copy is there in both languages and two radio buttons pick one (`:has`); the theme is the system's, turned the other way by a checkbox; posts are plain `<details>`, opened by the browser when the address points inside one (an anchor at the start of the text); each layer ends with "previous · …" and "next · …" links in place of the poise gauge, and the ocean has "next · menu". The page sends a browser without JavaScript there (`<noscript>` refresh, in the page and in every link-preview page, keeping the address), and `?raw` (or `&raw`) on any address does the same with it. It is `noindex`, with the home page as its canonical address.

## Rules

- The first view is readable within a second, on a slow phone network: nothing in `<head>` may block rendering (no external stylesheet or script), the first view's text needs nothing from the network, and nothing heavy runs on the main thread before the statement has faded in. Measured under Lighthouse's mobile throttling (150 ms round trips, 1.6 Mbps, 4x CPU) with Brotli: the statement reads at about 0.7 s on either renderer, connection setup aside. Changing the statement's copy or the first view's characters means rerunning `tools/fonts/build.py`.

- Keep three.js pinned at 0.147.0. It is the last release that ships the UMD build together with `examples/js`; moving to ES modules needs an import map and a rework of the script tags.
- Every piece of visible copy exists in English and French. Add both, or neither. Blog posts are the exception: Matei's own words, in English only, never translated or edited. Link previews are English.
- After changing the statement, a panel's opening lines, any copy, the views, the styles or the posts, run `node tools/meta/build.js` (it also writes the raw page; `wrangler.jsonc` runs it before each deploy).
- The site works without JavaScript, through the raw page. Keep the raw page free of script (its build refuses one) and keep its tree the page's.
- Keep it fast on integrated GPUs: 30 fps with the GPU under about 55% of the frame budget. Check any shader change against frame time, not just looks: `#diag` on real hardware, `tools/shader-cost` for the WGSL. Check any WGSL change with `tools/safari-wgsl` too.
- `nav.js` is plain JavaScript checked as strict TypeScript, no build: `tsc --noEmit --strict --allowJs --checkJs --target es2022 --lib es2022,dom nav.js` must pass. Keep it small.
- The UI is instant: nothing it does waits on the scene or on a frame (a panel lays out and starts its 0.22 s fade at once), and no transition has a delay. Keep keyboard focus visible. The scene and the UI look and move the same everywhere: no `prefers-reduced-motion` variants.
- Site copy: plain sentences, no em dashes.

## Design

- Palette: mist sky `#E6F0F3`, ink `#12313D`; night ink `#D7ECF2` on `#071824`. The water colour comes from the shaders; `--scene` only mirrors it for the fallback.
- Type: Newsreader italic for the voice; JetBrains Mono, lowercase, for the corner controls.
- The phone's picture on every screen: the camera has the phone's focal length, 60 degrees over the 812 CSS pixels of a phone's web view (`PHONE_F`), and renders 1.2 pixels per CSS pixel, so a CSS pixel shows the same piece of sea, sampled as finely, everywhere. A taller window sees more, not bigger. Very wide windows lengthen the focal length so the view stays within 100 degrees across.
- The camera pitches down until the horizon sits 38% down the screen (`HORIZON`), with the statement resting just above it (CSS `bottom: calc(62vh + ...)`; keep the two in step). The eye is 1.25 m above the water (`EYE`).
- Two suns: the real one (55 degrees up, front right, `LOOK.sun`) lights the water and makes the caustics; a glint sun sits where the phone has it, 3.5 degrees above the top of the phone's 60-degree frame (25.6 degrees up, `GLINT_EL`), and only makes the glitter path and the glow in the sky. Its disc is never drawn; on frames taller than the phone's, its glow shows at the top edge. The moon (12 degrees up, left) does both at night and is in frame, drawn from its photograph (see Sky).
- Mark: a lion's face in a sunburst mane (Sun in Aries, Moon in Leo). The source is `assets/lion.svg`; an inline copy lives in `index.html`.

## Preview and deploy

- Preview: `python3 -m http.server 8000`, then open http://localhost:8000.
- Deploy: Cloudflare Workers, static assets only (`wrangler.jsonc`: `npx wrangler deploy` runs `tools/meta/build.js` first, and `.assetsignore` keeps the tooling out of the upload); any static host works too (Cloudflare compresses with Brotli and reads `_headers`: 30-day caching for the font files) with the domain's DNS at Porkbun. `tools/` is development only. A `cv.pdf` placed next to `index.html` can be linked from the contact panel.
