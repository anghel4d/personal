# Writing Fiction He retreated into the writing of fiction because he didn't know how to say anything Real. Every word carelelessly wrought out of his fingers as they danced across the ~~page~~ ~~typewriter~~ ~~keyboard~~ touch screen, digging a trench deeper and deeper, further and further away from the menacing light of truth beating down from the sky. He could hide here, he thought; he could scurry away and make a home of this forever. Under mole-light he wrote, what was the plight of the author in the era of truth? Everything is known. Every fact is trivially checked. Just ask The Computer, he thought. He thought about Aristophanes, about Herodotus, about Tacitus, about their inventions and all the rest of them. How beautifully their falsities bloomed! The reader of antiquities, that font of everlasting civilization, was a reader of lies. Paralyzed, he thought. Paralyzed by the truth that now loomed over everything, conspiring to kill Culture once and for all. So he wrote fiction, he did, because fiction makes a cloak of being false. All events in this story are a work of fiction. Any similarity to real people, places, facts, ideas, or emotions is purely coincidental. A truly great author, the likes of which I've never read, makes something beautiful out of mundaneity. But isn't the modern condition already a tired tale? Overwrought, yet untempered, abortive, underdevelopped. A story of arrested development. Pulp writers whose prints ended up on recycled wood-chippings, *they* were the ones meant to write fantastical things. Action, horror ,adventure! Or, to put it briefly, "pulp". Low-brow garbage for the mass. The value of the medium of transcription is synonymous with the value of the work. What was HIS value, then, he who wrote in bits and bytes, effortlessly adding to the sea of loghorrea entirely for free? His thought was interrupted by the loud CRASH of shattering glass, as a flashbang flew through the window before exploding on the floor of that room in which he wrote. Manuscripts took flight, crinkling scattering about the entire space; the dust suddenly kicked up filled his lungs, bringing about violent coughs with his ears all ringing and his head pounding. Except there were no manuscripts, because he wrote on The Computer. He didn't even have a printer. There was no flashbang either. He was having a stroke and he died. At this, he attained enlightenment.
